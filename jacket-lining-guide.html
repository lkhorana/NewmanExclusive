Bespoke Jacket Lining Guide: Full Lined vs. Half Lined vs. Summer vs. Winter

Choosing the right custom jacket goes beyond just picking a fabric. Understanding the internal construction — how a jacket is lined — makes all the difference for your comfort, style, and climate.

Here is a breakdown of the differences to help you select the ideal custom garment for your wardrobe.

Full Lined Jackets

The traditional standard for bespoke tailoring. The interior is completely covered with a lining fabric, giving the jacket structure, weight, and a smooth glide over shirts. Ideal for cooler weather and air-conditioned offices.

Half Lined Jackets

Features lining only across the upper back and shoulders, leaving the lower interior seams exposed. This maximizes breathability and flexibility without losing structural shape in the shoulders.

Summer Lightweight "Summer Breeze" Jackets

Crafted from open-weave, highly breathable fabrics like linen, tropical wool, or cotton. Often half lined or completely unlined to maximize airflow and keep you cool in high temperatures.

Winter Jackets

Constructed from heavier, dense cloths such as tweed, flannel, or cashmere blends, paired with a full lining to trap heat and block cold winds.

Why Do Half Lined Jackets Cost More?

It sounds counterintuitive: less material should mean a lower price, right?

In bespoke tailoring, the opposite is often true. Because a half lined jacket leaves the interior seams entirely exposed, tailoring precision is everything.

Without a lining to hide the inner workings, master tailors must spend significantly more time ensuring absolute perfection. Every single internal seam is meticulously bound or piped, and raw edges are hand-tacked. This intensive hand-craftsmanship increases labor time considerably, resulting in a superior, lightweight piece of art.
